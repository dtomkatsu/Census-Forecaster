# Hawaii Tax Policy Impact Analysis: 2017 vs 2024 Brackets
**Revenue Impact of Post-TCJA Tax Changes on 2026 Projections**

**Date:** October 27, 2025  
**Analysis:** Comparison of 2026 income projections under different tax policy scenarios

---

## 🎯 Executive Summary

**Hawaii's tax policy changes between 2017 and 2024 would reduce 2026 state income tax revenue by $937 million (-18.4%) compared to the pre-TCJA era.**

### Key Findings
- **2017 Policy Revenue:** $5.09 billion (scaled to full population)
- **2024 Policy Revenue:** $4.16 billion (scaled to full population)  
- **Revenue Loss:** $937 million (-18.4%)
- **Primary Driver:** Doubled standard deductions reducing taxable income

---

## 📊 Detailed Revenue Comparison

### Overall Impact (Scaled to Full Hawaii Population)

| Metric | 2017 Policy | 2024 Policy | Difference | % Change |
|--------|-------------|-------------|------------|----------|
| **Total Tax Revenue** | $5.09B | $4.16B | **-$937M** | **-18.4%** |
| **Average Tax per Filer** | $7,275 | $5,937 | -$1,338 | -18.4% |
| **Median Tax per Filer** | $4,241 | $3,180 | -$1,061 | -25.0% |
| **Average Effective Rate** | 6.03% | 4.34% | -1.69pp | -28.1% |

### Standard Deduction Impact

| Filing Status | 2017 Deduction | 2024 Deduction | Increase |
|---------------|----------------|----------------|----------|
| **Joint/Surviving Spouse** | $4,400 | $8,800 | **+$4,400** |
| **Head of Household** | $3,212 | $6,424 | **+$3,212** |
| **Single/Married Separate** | $2,200 | $4,400 | **+$2,200** |

**Result:** Standard deductions **doubled across all filing statuses**, significantly reducing taxable income.

## 🏛️ Policy Implications

### Revenue Impact Context
- **$937M reduction** represents approximately **18.4%** of Hawaii's projected income tax revenue
- This is equivalent to the state's entire **Department of Education** annual budget
- Revenue loss could impact funding for:
  - Public education programs
  - Infrastructure projects  
  - Social services
  - Emergency reserves

### Distributional Effects
1. **Progressive Relief:** Lower-income taxpayers received proportionally larger benefits
2. **Broad-Based Impact:** All income groups and filing statuses benefited
3. **Simplification:** Higher standard deductions reduce itemization complexity

### Economic Considerations
- **Stimulus Effect:** $937M in tax cuts provides economic stimulus
- **Competitiveness:** Lower tax burden may attract residents and businesses
- **Trade-offs:** Reduced revenue limits government service capacity

## 🔧 Methodology Notes

### Data Sources
- **Base Income:** 2026 projections from ensemble growth model (ACS + BLS)
- **Tax Brackets:** Hawaii Department of Taxation historical data
- **Sample Size:** 34,887 PUMS tax units scaled to ~700,000 Hawaii filers

### Policy Scenarios
1. **2017 Policy:** Pre-TCJA brackets and standard deductions
2. **2024 Policy:** Post-TCJA brackets and standard deductions
3. **Income Base:** Identical 2026 projected incomes for both scenarios

### Limitations
- Analysis excludes itemized deductions (would reduce 2017 revenue further)
- No behavioral responses modeled (taxpayer behavior changes)
- Credits not included (would affect both scenarios)
- Capital gains excluded from income projections
