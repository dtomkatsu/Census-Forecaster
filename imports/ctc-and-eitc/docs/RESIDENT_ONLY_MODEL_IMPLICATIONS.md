# Resident-Only Model: Critical Implications for Revenue Projections

## Executive Summary

**CRITICAL INSIGHT**: Our PUMS-based model estimates **RESIDENT-ONLY** revenue, but Hawaii DOT fiscal year data includes **TOTAL** revenue (residents + non-residents + part-year residents).

**Non-resident revenue**: ~8.8% of total (~$289M in FY 2025)

This fundamentally changes how we interpret our model's accuracy and Act 46 impact estimates.

## The Resident vs Total Revenue Split

### FY 2025 Revenue Breakdown

| Component | Revenue ($M) | % of Total |
|-----------|--------------|------------|
| **Total Revenue** | $3,288 | 100.0% |
| Non-Resident Revenue | $289 | 8.8% |
| **Resident Revenue** | **$2,999** | **91.2%** |

### What This Means for Our Model

**Our ensemble model produces**: $3,298M (residents only)

**Should compare to**: $2,999M (FY 2025 residents only)

**NOT**: $3,288M (FY 2025 total)

## Revised Model Accuracy Assessment

### Before Resident Adjustment (WRONG)

```
Our ensemble net:      $3,298M
FY 2026 total:         $3,288M
Difference:            +$10M (+0.3%) ✅ Appeared excellent!
```

**Problem**: We were comparing residents to total (apples to oranges)

### After Resident Adjustment (CORRECT)

```
Our ensemble net:      $3,298M (residents)
FY 2026 residents:     $2,999M (91.2% of $3,288M)
Difference:            +$299M (+10.0%) ⚠️ Moderate overestimate
```

**Reality**: Our model overestimates resident revenue by **10%**

## Growth Rate Implications

### FY Actual Trends (Resident-Only)

```
FY 2022 Resident: $3,429M (91.2% of $3,760M peak)
FY 2025 Resident: $2,999M (91.2% of $3,288M)
CAGR: -4.4% (declining from peak)
```

### Our Model Growth (Resident-Only)

```
CY 2022 Net: $2,480M (residents, after credits)
CY 2026 Net: $3,298M (residents, after credits)
CAGR: +7.4%
```

### The Disconnect

**Difference**: +11.8 percentage points

**Our model projects strong growth** (+7.4%) while **FY shows decline** (-4.4%)

**Why?**
1. FY 2022 was exceptional peak (COVID recovery + capital gains)
2. Our CY 2022 baseline is more normalized
3. BUT our growth rate is still too optimistic for post-peak environment

## Act 46 Impact Recalculation

### Official Act 46 Impact (Total Revenue)

```
FY 2026 Pre-Policy (Total):  $3,288M
Act 46 Impact:                -$597M
FY 2026 Post-Policy (Total):  $2,691M
```

### Act 46 as % of Resident Revenue

```
Official Impact:              -$597M
Resident Revenue Base:        $2,999M
Impact as % of Residents:     -19.9%
```

### Our Corrected Act 46 Estimate

**Using official -19.9% rate**:
```
Pre-Act 46 (residents):       $3,298M
Act 46 Impact (-19.9%):       -$657M
Post-Act 46 (residents):      $2,641M
```

**Add back non-residents**:
```
Post-Act 46 (residents):      $2,641M
Non-residents (8.8% of pre):  $289M
Post-Act 46 (total):          $2,931M
```

**Compare to official total**:
```
Our total projection:         $2,931M
Official post-policy:         $2,691M
Difference:                   +$240M (+8.9%)
```

**Still overestimating**, but much closer than the 2.4x overestimate we calculated before!

## What We Got Wrong Before

### Mistake 1: Comparing Residents to Total

**What we did**:
- Our ensemble net ($3,298M residents) vs FY total ($3,288M)
- Concluded we were within 1% ✅ (WRONG!)

**What we should have done**:
- Our ensemble net ($3,298M residents) vs FY residents ($2,999M)
- Actual error: +10.0% ⚠️

### Mistake 2: Act 46 Impact on Wrong Base

**What we did**:
- Compared our inflated $4,872M baseline to $3,414M post-Act 46
- Got -$1,458M impact (2.4x too high)

**What we should have done**:
- Use our ensemble net ($3,298M residents)
- Apply official -19.9% impact rate
- Get -$657M impact (vs official -$597M on total)
- Difference: +$60M (+10%), much more reasonable

## Corrected Recommendations

### 1. Our Model Overestimates by ~10%

**Finding**: Our ensemble net ($3,298M) is 10% higher than FY 2026 resident target ($2,999M)

**Likely Causes**:
1. **Growth rate too optimistic** (+7.4% vs -4.4% FY actual)
2. **Income projections too high** (using 2018-2021 high-growth period)
3. **Capital gains may still be elevated** (post-COVID surge)
4. **Filing status distribution** may differ from actual

**Solutions**:
- Reduce ensemble growth rate from 7.4% to 2-3%
- Anchor to FY 2025 resident revenue ($2,999M)
- Apply modest 2% growth → $3,059M target for 2026
- This brings us within 5% of target

### 2. Act 46 Impact is More Reasonable

**Previous estimate**: -$1,458M (-29.9%) ❌ Way too high
**Corrected estimate**: -$657M (-19.9%) ⚠️ Close but still 10% high
**Official estimate**: -$597M (-18.2% of total)

**Interpretation**:
- Our 10% overestimate on Act 46 impact matches our 10% baseline overestimate
- If we fix the baseline, Act 46 estimate will also improve
- The -19.9% rate is reasonable (official is -18.2% of total, which is -19.9% of residents after accounting for 8.8% non-resident share)

### 3. Growth Rate Still Needs Adjustment

**Current**: +7.4% CAGR (residents)
**FY Trend**: -4.4% CAGR (residents, from peak)
**Realistic**: +2% CAGR (modest growth from normalized FY 2025)

**Recommended Target**:
```
FY 2025 Resident:     $2,999M
Apply 2% growth:      $3,059M (2026 residents)
Add 8.8% non-resident: $3,354M (2026 total)
```

**This matches** the realistic estimate we calculated before!

### 4. Always Specify Resident vs Total

**From now on, all revenue estimates must specify**:

✅ **CORRECT**:
- "Our model projects $3,298M in **resident** tax revenue"
- "To get total revenue, add ~9% for non-residents: $3,610M"

❌ **INCORRECT**:
- "Our model projects $3,298M" (ambiguous - resident or total?)
- "Comparing to FY $3,288M" (mixing resident and total)

## Key Takeaways

### 1. Resident vs Total is Critical

**Non-resident revenue** (~8.8%) may seem small, but it **completely changes** how we interpret model accuracy:
- Ignoring it: We looked 0.3% accurate ✅
- Accounting for it: We're 10% overestimate ⚠️

### 2. Our Model Needs Recalibration (But Not Major Surgery)

**Good News**:
- ✅ 10% overestimate is reasonable for a microsimulation model
- ✅ The methodology is sound
- ✅ Most of the error comes from growth rate assumptions

**Bad News**:
- ❌ 10% is too high for policy analysis
- ❌ Growth rate of 7.4% is unrealistic post-peak
- ❌ Need to reduce to 2-3% to match FY normalization

### 3. Act 46 Impact Much More Reasonable

**Before**: Overestimating by 144% (completely wrong)
**After**: Overestimating by 10% (acceptable with caveats)

This 10% matches our baseline 10% overestimate, suggesting:
- Fix the baseline → Act 46 estimate automatically improves
- The -19.9% impact rate is reasonable
- Main issue is the baseline being too high

### 4. FY 2025 is the Right Anchor

**Instead of**:
- Using FY 2022 peak ($3,760M) → Creates false decline narrative
- Using our CY 2022 baseline → Different measurement period

**Use**:
- FY 2025 actual ($3,288M total, $2,999M residents)
- Most recent actual data
- Already normalized from peak
- Project modest growth from this base
